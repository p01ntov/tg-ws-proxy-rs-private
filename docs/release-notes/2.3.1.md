# v2.3.1

Connect latency only. Four places where the routing ladder either spent more
of its timeout budget than it was given, or put a path known to be failing
ahead of one that works. No protocol, flag or environment-variable changes.

## Why

The bridge itself was never the slow part — once a WebSocket is up, a packet
costs one AES-CTR pass and one frame. What clients experience as high ping is
almost always the *connect*: how long the proxy spends on doomed attempts
before it reaches a path that answers. Telegram sitting in "Connecting..." is
the visible form of it, and every one of the fixes below is about the arithmetic
of that walk rather than the data path.

## Fixed

- **`--ws-connect-timeout` was spent twice per hostname.** `connect_ws_with_path`
  passed the full duration to the TCP connect *and* then wrapped the TLS
  handshake and WebSocket upgrade in another timeout of the same length. An
  address that answered its SYN and then stalled cost 2x the configured value,
  and since a DC has two hostnames, `--ws-connect-timeout 10` could hold a
  client for 40s before the next tier was tried. One deadline now covers the
  whole attempt: the handshake gets whatever the connect did not spend.

- **A TCP connect timeout re-dialled the same address.** Both hostnames for a DC
  dial the same `--dc-ip`; only the SNI and `Host` header differ, and both are
  chosen after the TCP handshake completes. So nothing answering at that address
  is a verdict on the address, not on the name — trying the second hostname
  could only burn a second full timeout. The hostname loop now stops on a
  connect timeout, and keeps iterating on every other outcome (a refusal or a
  redirect *is* per-hostname).

- **A DC in its WebSocket cooldown still went first.** `WS_FAIL` shortened the
  probe to `--ws-fail-probe-timeout` but left it at the head of the ladder, so
  for the whole 30s window every connection paid that probe before reaching a
  path that works. The direct attempt now goes last during the window, on the
  same self-healing shape the IP cooldown already used: it is still tried if
  every fallback is gone, so a cooldown set by a passing glitch is cleared by
  the next connection that succeeds.

- **The FakeTLS upstream handshake was unbounded.** With `--mtproto-proxy` and
  an `ee` secret, `--upstream-connect-timeout` covered only the TCP connect.
  The ClientHello write, the server's fake handshake and the init record that
  follow are reads of the peer's own making, so an upstream that answered its
  SYN and then went quiet hung there with nothing to break it. Because this
  tier sits ahead of the raw-TCP last resort, that stall took the last resort
  down with it and the client never connected at all. One deadline now covers
  reaching a usable upstream, not just its handshake.

## Changed

- A pool hit is checked before the fallback ladder rather than after the
  cooldown branches. A pooled connection is already past its handshake, and
  only a fresh direct connect clears `WS_FAIL` — so a background refill could
  succeed and leave the flag set, at which point the reordering above would
  have stepped over a live pre-warmed connection in favour of a Cloudflare
  tier. Unchanged during an `IP_FAIL` window, where the address itself is the
  suspect and a pooled connection over a blackholed one can pass the liveness
  check and still be dead.

## Not changed

Several plausible-looking wins were measured and rejected rather than shipped:

- Batching the upload direction with `feed()` + one `flush()` saves nothing —
  `MsgSplitter` emits a part only on packet completion, so one client read
  yields at most one frame for any packet larger than the read buffer.
- A global deadline across the whole ladder, or racing tiers happy-eyeballs
  style, would either cut live relays short or light up every fallback at once —
  which is exactly the traffic pattern the fallback tiers exist to avoid on a
  censored network.
- A per-record cooldown on the Cloudflare proxy tier would remove retries from
  the tier standing between the user and blocked TCP. The repo's own notes
  record a tester's raw-TCP share going from 5.9% to 11.4% when one CF retry
  was dropped.
